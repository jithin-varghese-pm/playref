# Imposter rule cards (Playref standard rules)

Written in our own words for Playref. Imposter is a folk party game with no official rulebook, so these are Playref's standard rules, chosen from the research in reports/Imposter game rules.md (not committed). Names avoided on purpose: Spyfall, The Chameleon, Undercover, Mr. White.

Format: each card starts with `## I<number>: <title>`, then one `Official:` line (the Playref standard) and one `House:` line (common variants).

## I1: Players
Official: 3 to 10 players, one phone passed around. It plays best with 4 or more.
House: Some groups play with more than 10. It still works, but rounds get long.

## I2: How many imposters
Official: 1 imposter for 3 to 6 players, 2 imposters for 7 to 10. The screen never says how many there are, and imposters do not know about each other.
House: Some groups always play with 2 imposters, or let the imposters know each other.

## I3: What everyone sees
Official: Everyone sees the category. Everyone except the imposter also sees the secret word. The imposter sees "You are the Imposter" plus the category, on a card that looks exactly like everyone else's.
House: In a harder version the imposter sees no category. In the decoy version the imposter gets a similar word and is not told.

## I4: Dealing the cards
Official: Pass the phone to each player in turn. They hold the button to see their card, and it hides as soon as they let go. Nobody else looks. If you forget your word, ask for the phone and peek again on the same screen.
House: None common.

## I5: Who is the imposter
Official: The app picks at random, but fairly: nobody is the imposter twice in a row, and people who have not been the imposter for a while are more likely to be picked.
House: None common.

## I6: Clues
Official: Two rounds of clues. The app picks who starts, and it is never an imposter. Then go clockwise. Each player says exactly one word that hints at the secret word. You cannot say the word itself, a form of it, the same word in another language, or a clue someone already gave. Everyone must give a clue; you cannot pass.
House: Some groups allow short phrases of up to 3 words, ban close synonyms too, or use a timer of about 20 seconds per clue.

## I7: Talking
Official: After the clue rounds, everyone talks openly. Anyone can ask questions, defend themselves or bluff, including the imposter. There is no timer.
House: Some groups use a 60 second discussion timer.

## I8: Voting
Official: Voting happens only after the clue rounds. On "3, 2, 1" everyone points at the same moment, the imposter included. The player with the most votes is accused. More than half is not needed.
House: Some groups vote one by one.

## I9: Ties
Official: If the vote is tied, each tied player gives one more clue word, then everyone votes again between the tied players only. If it is still tied, the imposter wins.
House: Some groups do a straight revote, call it a draw, or let a tie go to the imposter straight away.

## I10: The reveal and the last guess
Official: The accused player shows their card. If they are not an imposter, the imposters win. If they are, they get one spoken guess at the secret word. Right guess: the imposter wins. Wrong guess: the group wins. The same thing in any language counts as right ("aloo" for "potato"). A broader or narrower word does not ("vegetable", "sweet potato").
House: Some groups play with no last guess, so a caught imposter loses straight away.

## I11: Guessing early
Official: An imposter may stop the game at any time and say "I'm the imposter, the word is ...". Right guess: the imposter wins. Wrong guess: the imposter loses. It cannot be taken back.
House: None common.

## I12: Saying the word by mistake
Official: If a non-imposter says the secret word, a form of it, or a translation of it during the clues, the round is cancelled. Deal a new word and nobody scores.
House: Some groups just carry on.

## I13: Two imposters
Official: With 2 imposters, the group must catch both. When one is caught, they make their last guess, then the group votes once more straight away, with no new clues, to find the other. Any imposter who escapes or guesses right wins for the imposters.
House: Some groups keep playing rounds and vote out one player each round until all imposters are out or only one other player is left.

## I14: Winning and points
Official: The group wins by catching every imposter and surviving the last guess. The imposters win by escaping the vote, winning a final tie, or guessing the word. Points are optional: an imposter who escapes gets 2, a caught imposter who guesses right gets 1, and when the imposters lose every other player gets 1. Play until everyone has been the imposter once.
House: Many groups skip points and play for bragging rights.
