# Classic UNO rule cards

Written in our own words for Playref, an unofficial fan project. Checked against Mattel's Classic UNO rules sheet (GDJ85, 112-card deck with Wild Shuffle Hands and Wild Customizable cards). UNO is a trademark of Mattel.

Format: each card starts with `## R<number>: <title>`, then one `Official:` line and one `House:` line. `House:` says how groups often play it differently, or "None common."

## R1: Goal and winning
Official: Each round, try to be first to play all your cards. The round winner scores points for the cards still in everyone else's hands. Rounds add up, and the first player to reach 500 points wins the game. Another allowed way: keep a running total of the points each player is left holding, and when someone reaches 500, the player with the lowest total wins.
House: Many groups just play one round and the first person out wins.

## R2: Setting up
Official: Everyone draws a card. The highest number deals, and cards with symbols count as zero. The dealer shuffles and deals 7 cards to each player. The rest go face down as the draw pile. The top card is turned over to start the discard pile. The player to the dealer's left goes first.
House: None common.

## R3: When the first card turned over is an action card
Official: Draw Two: the first player draws 2 and misses their turn. Reverse: the dealer plays first and play goes to the right. Skip: the first player is skipped, so the next player starts. Wild: the first player chooses the colour and plays. Wild Draw Four: put it back in the deck and turn over another card. Wild Shuffle Hands or Wild Customizable: the first player chooses the colour.
House: None common.

## R4: What you can play
Official: On your turn, play one card that matches the top of the discard pile by colour, number or symbol. A Wild card can be played on anything. Only one card per turn.
House: Some groups let you play several cards of the same number at once. That is not in the Classic rules.

## R5: When you cannot play
Official: If you have no card that matches, draw one card from the draw pile. If that card can be played, you may play it right away. If not, your turn ends and play moves on. You draw only one card, not until you find one that works.
House: Many groups make you keep drawing until you get a card you can play. That is a house rule.

## R6: Drawing on purpose
Official: You may choose not to play, even when you have a playable card. Then you must draw one card. If that drawn card can be played, you may play it, but you may not play any other card from your hand that turn.
House: None common.

## R7: Draw Two
Official: The next player draws 2 cards and misses their turn. You can play a Draw Two on a card of the same colour or on another Draw Two. The player hit by a Draw Two cannot pass it on, because they lose their turn.
House: Stacking is the most common house rule: the next player adds their own Draw Two and the total keeps growing until someone cannot add one and draws it all. Some groups also stack Wild Draw Fours, or mix the two.

## R8: Reverse
Official: Play changes direction. You can play a Reverse on a card of the same colour or on another Reverse.
House: None common.

## R9: Skip
Official: The next player loses their turn. You can play a Skip on a card of the same colour or on another Skip.
House: None common.

## R10: Wild
Official: You choose the colour that play continues in. You may pick the colour that was already in play. You may play a Wild on your turn even if you have other cards you could play.
House: None common.

## R11: Wild Draw Four
Official: You choose the colour, and the next player draws 4 cards and loses their turn. You may only play it when you have no card in your hand that matches the colour of the discard pile. Having a card that matches only the number or symbol is fine. The player hit by it cannot answer with their own Wild Draw Four, because they lose their turn.
House: Many groups ignore the colour limit and let you play it any time. Many also stack Wild Draw Fours. Neither is in the Classic rules.

## R12: Challenging a Wild Draw Four
Official: If you think a Wild Draw Four was played on you while the player still had a card of the matching colour, you may challenge. They show you their hand. If they were wrong to play it, they draw the 4 cards instead of you. If they were allowed to play it, you draw the 4 cards plus 2 more, 6 in total.
House: Some groups skip challenges completely, and the next player just draws 4.

## R13: Wild Shuffle Hands
Official: Collect every card from every player's hand, shuffle them together and deal them all out, starting with the player to the left of the person who played it. Some players may end up with more or fewer cards than before. Play continues in the same direction. It is also a Wild, so the person who played it picks the colour.
House: None common.

## R14: Wild Customizable cards
Official: Write any house rule you like on these cards, as long as the other players agree. Before the game, decide whether to use 1, 2 or all 3 of them. Each is also a Wild, so you can play it even if you have other playable cards, and you pick the colour.
House: This card is the official place for house rules in Classic UNO.

## R15: Saying UNO
Official: When you play your second-to-last card, you must say "UNO" to show you have one card left. If you do not, and someone catches you before the next player begins their turn, you draw 2 cards. Once the next player has started their turn, it is too late to catch you.
House: Some groups add bigger penalties, or let anyone catch you at any time.

## R16: Going out
Official: The round ends as soon as someone plays their last card. You may go out on any card, including an action card. If the last card is a Draw Two or a Wild Draw Four, the next player still draws those cards, and they count when points are added up. If the draw pile runs out before anyone goes out, shuffle the discard pile to make a new draw pile and keep playing.
House: Some groups do not allow going out on an action card. That is not in the Classic rules.

## R17: Points
Official: Number cards score their face value. Draw Two, Reverse and Skip score 20 points each. Wild and Wild Draw Four score 50 points each. Wild Shuffle Hands and Wild Customizable score 40 points each.
House: None common.

## R19: Cards in the deck
Official: The deck has 112 cards. Number cards 0 to 9 come in four colours: red, yellow, green and blue. Draw Two, Reverse and Skip come in those four colours too. The Wild cards (Wild, Wild Draw Four, Wild Shuffle Hands and Wild Customizable) have no colour of their own: they are the black cards. There is no black number card. After a Wild, play continues in the colour its player chose, so the next card must match that colour or be another Wild.
House: None common.

## R18: Common house rules that are not in the Classic rules
Official: The Classic rules have none of these: stacking Draw Twos or Wild Draw Fours; swapping hands when a 7 or 0 is played; jumping in out of turn with an identical card; drawing until you can play; playing several cards in one turn; banning going out on an action card.
House: Any of these is fine if everyone agrees before the game starts. A Wild Customizable card is a good way to add one.
